# Executive memo: Structured Products

## Retrocession Review

### Background

Based on typical market retrocession rates (1–5% of notional, split between one-off and recurring annual fees) applied to an estimated structured product strategy of more than \$50M executed over 23 years, the theoretical retrocession pool embedded in this strategy could plausibly range from \$500,000 to over \$2,500,000 — before any determination of who was entitled to receive it. This proposal is the first step in establishing where in that range the Trust's actual exposure falls, and who received it.

**Time-sensitive:** the 23 vintage ISINs (2003–2007) are at or past typical bank record-retention windows (commonly 10 years). Every quarter of delay reduces the probability of document recovery — and with it, the recoverable value.

### How retrocessions work

Issuers bake a global fee pool—typically 1–5% of notional —directly into a structured product's issue price, invisible to the investor as a separate charge. This pool is split downstream: a placement fee to the distributing bank, smaller custodial fees, and sometimes an advisory fee. Critically, the issuer also pays a retrocession, upfront and/or as an annual trailer, directly to a third party—often the distributing bank, but potentially the advisor (wealth manager) itself—through a backchannel that never appears on the client's statement, functioning much like a kickback for directing flow. The key unknown isn't whether this pool existed, but its size and who actually received it, since Trust / wealth manager entitlement would raise real disclosure and consent questions. Recovering the term sheet only defines the *theoretical* maximum size of this pool, not the actual amount paid or its recipient; resolving that requires a direct request to the paying institutions—particularly Swiss banks, where regulatory obligations can compel retrocession disclosure. Light Analytics estimates the pool's likely size using market assumptions; Complete Analytics pursues document recovery as a precursor to that direct disclosure request.

### Proposal

We propose a two-tier engagement to quantify total strategy cost and isolate the retrocession component: Tier 1 (Light Analytics) and Tier 2 (Complete Analytics).

|  | Tier 1 — Light Analytics | Tier 2 — Complete Analytics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Coverage** | All 29 ISINs | Subset with recoverable term sheets (6 confirmed; up to 23 vintages, subject to recovery) |
| **Method** | Heuristic/theoretical cost modelling using market data & financial mathematics | Contractual fee extraction from recovered term sheets, reconciled to bank statements |
| **Output** | Estimated total cost, retro range (low/base/high), cost component split | Auditable, itemised fee waterfall confirmed against source documents |
| **Confidence** | Indicative — model-based | High — evidence-based |
| **Use case** | Board reporting, initial prioritisation, negotiation leverage | Formal dispute, legal recovery, trustee disclosure |
| **Deliverable** | Excel model + summary tab (ISIN-level costs, sensitivity, assumptions) | Excel model + PDF audit report + source document repository |
| **Timeline** | 2 weeks | 4 weeks (post document recovery) |
| **Fee** | CHF 20,000 | CHF 50,000 |

To put these in context: Tier 1 (CHF 20,000) represents approximately 0.04% of the more than \$50M notional under review — comparable to a standard forensic accounting scoping exercise — and is small relative to the low end of the theoretical retrocession estimated above (\$500,000+).

## Recommended Path

We suggest starting with Tier 1 across all 29 ISINs to establish a portfolio-wide cost baseline and identify the highest-exposure positions, then triggering Tier 2—prioritising the 6 ISINs with confirmed documentation, followed by vintage ISINs subject to document recovery. In parallel, legal confirmation of retrocession entitlement—whether it rests with the Trust or the bank—should proceed independently, as this determines the recovery strategy regardless of the analytics tier pursued.

We recommend confirming the proof-of-concept ISIN by July 31, 2026, so that any formal document request to distributing banks for the 2003–2007 vintage can be initiated before Q3 2026 quarter-end (September 30, 2026) — minimizing further erosion of an already timesensitive recovery window for these oldest positions.

## Next Step

The Trust has no basis to confirm whether retrocession income on this strategy was received, disclosed, or owed to the Trust — a gap that time will not resolve. Vintage documentation becomes less recoverable each quarter, and entitlement remains an open fiduciary question despite inaction. Commissioning this analysis is the only way to convert that question into a documented, closed matter.
